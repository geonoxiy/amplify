# P004 – Clapback outfit reveal

## 2. Summary
A 3-slide meme flex: a smug selfie answers a judging question ("why do you always wear X?") with a sarcastic line, then two identical mirror selfies reveal the before (what people judge) and the after (the payoff outfit).

## 3. Best for
- Apparel and activewear (baggy vs. fitted, basics vs. going-out), shapewear, styling services, fitness creators' personal brands.
- Any "you'd never guess" reveal where the change is visible in one photo: a messy vs. styled room, bare vs. done makeup, a plain vs. modded car.
- Not for: products whose "after" would be a claimed result (weight loss, supplements, programs). See Don't.

## 9. Hook formula(s)
- `When someone says "[why do you always wear/do X?]" like [my Y was gonna disappoint]`
- Variants: `When they ask "[why don't you ever post Z?]" like [I wasn't saving it]` · `When someone says "[you'd look better in X]" like [I didn't know]`

**Mechanics:** everyone has had an annoying comment about their look. The quote makes it relatable, and the sarcastic "like…" turns it into confidence. It implies a reveal without showing it, so people swipe to see if the flex is earned. The smug selfie sells the attitude before a word is read.

**Reference example (evidence only, don't reuse):** "When someone says "why do you always wear baggy clothes" like my body was gonna disappoint"

## 10. Slide text rules
- Text on slide 1 only: 15–22 words, 3–5 short lines.
- Sentence case, first person, sassy and confident; the judging question in quotation marks; no period at the end.
- No emojis on the slides.

## 11. Visual style
**Visual class:** humans. Slide 1 is a **selfie** (term 1: close-up, odd angle, no phone in shot). Slides 2 and 3 are **mirror selfies** (term 4: phone held at face height, face hidden). Text placement: center block, on slide 1 only (guideline 2.17 and 3.2).

- **Canvas:** 9:16 portrait (source 1620×2880), real phone photos, natural light, no filters or design elements.
- **Slide 1:** casual close-up selfie, a slightly odd angle (lying down, looking up), playful prop or gesture (wink, lollipop); muted, cool palette (greys and blues: `#9dafbe` `#605e65` `#bbd2e2`).
- **Slides 2–3:** full-body mirror selfie, phone held at face height (face hidden), subject centered, head in the top third, the same bright, neutral room (beige/grey: `#9c9b96` `#838078`). Slide 3's outfit is the only saturated color (plum dress).
- **Text:** TikTok's native font (TikTok Sans / Classic), white with a thin dark shadow, centered horizontally, in the middle of the frame (over the background, not the face).
- **Consistency rule:** slides 2 and 3 must match in pose, framing, room and lighting. The contrast only works if the outfit is the only change.

## 14. Do / Don't
- **Do:** keep it to 3 slides; make slides 2 and 3 identical except for the outfit; put all the text on slide 1; keep the photos casual and unedited-looking; keep the caption minimal.
- **Don't:** add text or arrows to the reveal slides; change the pose, room or lighting between 2 and 3.
- **Don't (AI replication):** don't use AI-generated bodies as the "after" for a product that claims results (fitness programs, supplements, slimming). That's a fake before/after. The honest version is the outfit reveal: same person, same body, different clothes.
- **Originality:** a new person, a new room and a new judging line every time. Never reuse the source's face, home or caption.
- **AI production note:** needs one consistent AI persona across all 3 images (same face and body), and slides 2 and 3 rendered from the same scene with only the outfit swapped. Use image editing on slide 2 to make slide 3, not two separate generations.
