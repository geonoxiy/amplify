## 2. Summary
A problem-hook carousel that names the muscles behind a body-part goal, then gives one illustrated slide per muscle with 2 to 3 exercise options, drawn with a recurring faceless mascot; often ends with an eBook CTA.

## 3. Best for
- Educational "fix my weak [part]" content that breaks a goal into parts and gives 2 to 3 options per part (muscle groups → exercises; skin concerns → ingredients; budget areas → tools).
- Accounts with a recurring illustrated character or diagram style, and a digital product to sell at the end.
- A single muscle or single topic also works: split it into its jobs, one slide each (run-01, upper traps: lifting, pulling, holding, overhead press), with the same anatomy overview first.
- Not for: news, opinion, single-item deep dives, or topics that don't split into parts.

## 4. Structure
6 to 8 slides. Roles in order: hook → [overview] → muscle picks (2 to 6 slides) → [CTA]. Brackets are optional (overview in 3 of 4 source posts, CTA in 2 of 4).

| # | Role | What happens | Text on screen (pattern, not the source's words) |
|---|------|--------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | Hook | Mascot posed to show the body part (flexing, curling, boxing stance), muscles color-coded when the topic is the back. | `[BODY PART] [PROBLEM WORD]?` or `YOUR [PART] LOOK [PROBLEM] / HERE'S HOW TO [OUTCOME]` or `STRUCTURE THE PERFECT [WORKOUT]` |

## 5. Product slot
Last slide in 2 of 4 posts (hard sell): the mascot holds a tablet with the eBook cover, no price and no link on the slide (the link is in the bio, a Linktree, product $19.99). The other 2 posts sell nothing. The caption never mentions the product. Soft-sell alternative for other accounts: keep the CTA slide but move the product into the last muscle slide.

## 9. Hook formula(s)
- `[BODY PART] [PROBLEM WORD]?` (2 words, question mark).
- `YOUR [BODY PART] LOOK [PROBLEM] / HERE'S HOW TO [OUTCOME]` (problem then promise, 3 lines, first line navy, last line blue).
- `STRUCTURE THE PERFECT [WORKOUT TYPE]` (instruction; italic condensed).
Mechanics: it names a visible insecurity in 2 to 3 words (identification) and promises the fix is inside (curiosity). The mascot's pose restates the problem so slide 1 works with the sound off. The big numbered muscle slides that follow give a clear swipe path.
Reference examples (evidence only): "WEAK BACK?", "SMALL ARMS?", "YOUR FOREARMS LOOK SMALL / HERE'S HOW TO LOOK MASSIVE IN A T-SHIRT", "STRUCTURE THE PERFECT PULL DAY".

## 10. Slide text rules
- Headings: Title Case, 1 to 4 words; rank as `#N`. Covers ALL CAPS.
- Body: none, or one explainer of up to 26 words (variant a). Exercise names in Title Case. Sets × reps as `2-3 Sets x 6-10 Reps`.
- Second person, imperative, no emoji, no periods on headings.
- Language: English. The source has typos; do not copy them.

## 12. Caption formula and hashtag pattern
- Caption: one line that restates the promise (`[Body part] guide!` / `Weak back? Then you have to train all aspects of it!`) + hashtags. No emoji, no CTA, no product mention.
- Hashtags: 3 to 5, topic-specific and niche (`#backday #gymtip #hypertrophy`); no broad tags like `#fyp`.

## 14. Do / Don't
- Do: keep the brand system fixed: beige card, mascot, logo top-left, the underline under every heading, the same two fonts.
- Do: draw every prop the tip mentions (the mascot holds the food, sits on the machine, wears the outfit).
- Do: leave the top-right of content slides empty; the eye goes heading → text → illustration.
- Don't: use photos, faces or a second character. The account never shows a real person.
- Don't: copy the source's typos (`positon`, `HUMANY`, `pefect`, `srarts`); they are errors, not style.
- Don't: reuse the source's exact wording, exercises order or drawings (originality check).
- Do: give 2 to 3 options per muscle, not one; the choice is the format.
- Don't: add a CTA to every post; half of the source posts have none.
