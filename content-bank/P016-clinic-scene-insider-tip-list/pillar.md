# P016 – Clinic-scene insider tip list

## 1. Source
Six Instagram Reels from a grid screenshot in the boss's test doc (page 1, "Here is one we wanted to copy", collected 2026-10-05). The link given was an Instagram login/keyword-search page (`explore/search/keyword/?q=snidin`), which only logged-in accounts can see, so no post was downloaded: everything below is read from the six grid tiles in `originals/screenshot/`. ## 2. Summary
A silent Reel of a masked, gloved professional working in their real workplace while a white-box hook and a long numbered tip list sit over them; the product hides as one tip in the middle of the list.

## 3. Best for
- Brands with a real professional setting that is true to them: a dental clinic for oral care, a pharmacy for a pharmacy, a salon for haircare, a gym floor for a coach.
- Save-worthy "quick wins" lists (habits, mistakes, myths, fun facts) where most tips are genuinely useful.
- Not for: anything where the scene would claim a qualification the brand or presenter doesn't have, or a prescription-only medicine as the product (see 14).

## 4. Structure
One continuous shot (no cuts seen), text on screen the whole time.

| # | Role | What happens | Text on screen (pattern, not the source's words) |
|---|------|--------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| 1 | Hook | Top box, readable before the list registers | One of the hook formulas in 9 |
| 2 | Tips 1–2 (or 1–3) | List starts with genuinely useful, specific tips | Short imperative or "X: do Y" lines, one idea each |
| 3 | Product tip | Tip #3–#5 (of 5–15): the product, same voice and length as the tips around it | `try [brand] [product] for [problem], [benefit], find it on [where]` |
| 4 | More tips | The list keeps going so the product isn't last | More quick wins |
| 5 | Closer (optional) | Last tip is a light punchline or a share prompt | e.g. a joke tip, "share this with someone who…" |

Variants seen: a ranked review list with scores per product (`[product]: [score]/10, [one-line verdict]`, the brand's product scored highest and placed 4th of 5), and an interactive trick list (06: numbered "facts" that make the viewer try something, skip a number on purpose, then call it out).

## 5. Product slot
Always inside the list, never in the hook: position 3, 4 or 5 of 5–15 items, written exactly like the other tips (a problem, the product name, a benefit, where to buy, "on Amazon / AMZ"). No logo, no product shot in frame. Soft sell by camouflage: it reads as one more tip from someone who knows. In 01 the brand's product is the only one scored above 10 ("100/10") among four real competitor products.

## 9. Hook formula(s)
Fill-in-the-blank templates, one per mechanic seen:
- **Experience + disbelief:** `[N] years [doing the hard part of the job] and people still don't believe me when I tell them this...`
- **Insider gap:** `in case your [professional] never told you...` / `in case nobody told you before [starting X]...`
- **Forbidden knowledge:** `secrets even [professionals] won't tell you` (use only for harmless knowledge; never imply professionals hide health information)
- **Regret:** `i wish more people knew [this / this before X]`
- **Benefit + intensifier:** `small habits that [benefit] (big time)`
- **Interactive trick:** `[N] amazing fun facts`, then items that make the viewer try something

Hook mechanics: the scene supplies credibility before a word is read (mask, gloves, monitors, a patient); the hook adds a curiosity gap (something known inside the job that the viewer hasn't been told) and a promise of quick, usable payoff. The long list is a save trigger ("too much to remember, save it"), and the trick list is a comment trigger. Keep the source's register: plain, a little dramatic, first person or second person, ellipsis on the insider hooks.
Reference examples (evidence only): "8 years cleaning the dirtiest teeth and people still don't believe me when I tell them this...", "SMALL HABITS THAT SLOW AGING (BIG TIME)", "In case your dentist never told you...", "Secrets even doctors won't tell you", "I wish more people knew", "10 AMAZING FUN FACTS".

## 10. Slide text rules
Hook 4–16 words. List items 3–20 words, one idea each, imperative or `[problem]: [fix]`. 5–15 items. Second person ("your teeth", "you skipped number 5"). Plain words, a few specifics (times, numbers, named everyday items). Source uses title case or caps; **this project's rule overrides it: lowercase, no em dash, en dash, colon or semicolon** (so `problem: fix` becomes `problem, fix` or `for [problem], [fix]`). Emoji only as a closer (😂 in 06).

## 11. Visual style
- Class: humans (a worker at work in a real professional room). Not a selfie, not talking to camera.
- Scene (fixed): a real, working professional room of the brand's own kind, busy and lit by the room's own light (overhead clinic lights, monitor glow), equipment and screens behind; the worker in uniform with mask and gloves (face mostly covered, eyes down), framed waist up or closer, often slightly off-centre with a patient or work surface in the lower third.
- Text (fixed): white boxes, near-black text. Hook box at about y 7–14%, centred, 1–2 lines, bold sans (or condensed caps in 06). List below at about y 35–80%, either one white panel (02, 03) or a white strip per line (04, 06), sitting over the worker's chest; small type (about 2% of the height), left-aligned in panels, centred in strips. Box ranges estimated from the grid tiles. Rendered by `tools/boxlist.py`.
- Varies across posts: hook wording and mechanic, list length, panel vs. strips, font (01 uses a typewriter serif with outlined hook text), which worker and which task.

## 14. Do / Don't
- Do: set the scene in a real room of the brand's own kind, with the worker busy and their face mostly covered; open with one of the hook formulas in 9 in the source's register; make most tips genuinely useful and specific; put the product at #3–#5 in the same voice as the tips; keep text inside y 7–80%.
- Do: use the setting that carries the hook even when a persona is requested. The scene is the format here; a persona can be the worker, not move the post to their kitchen (owner's boss feedback, 2026-10-05).
- Don't: give a fictional or AI person a qualification on screen ("8 years as a dentist", "as a pharmacist") or a name badge; the hook speaks for the brand or uses the second-person insider formulas instead.
- Don't: copy the source's health claims. Several source tips are false or unsafe ("vitamin B2, fall asleep instantly", "drink less coke to conceive", "erases fine lines like magic in 7 days"); every tip in a new post must be true and general.
- Don't: name a prescription-only medicine, show its pack or pen, or make weight, body or outcome claims (UK law bans advertising prescription-only medicines to the public; see `brands/boltpharmacy.md`). For a pharmacy brand the product tip is the service.
