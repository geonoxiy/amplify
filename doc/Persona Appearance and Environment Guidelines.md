# Persona Appearance and Environment Guidelines

What to write down about an AI persona, and the world they live in, so that every render looks like the same real person, alive, in the same real home. A persona file (`personas/<id>.md`) is filled in from Parts 2 to 6; every shot gets a moment spec from Part 7.

## Part 1: Principles

### 1.1 Why each rule exists

* A moment, not a portrait: a real photo catches a person in the middle of something, reacting to something. An AI render shows a person posing for nobody: eyes on the lens, weight on both feet, even smile, flat light, clean clothes, still hair. Most of the AI look comes from that, not from the face. Every shot gets a cause (Part 7).
* Everything is caused: a glance has a target, a squint has a sun, a crease has a bend, hair moves because of wind or movement, a shoulder drops because of the bag on it. If nothing in the scene causes it, it shouldn't be there, and if the scene causes it, it must be there.
* Specific beats pretty: "straight nose with a small bump on the bridge" renders as a person; "nice nose" renders as a stock face. Every trait should be something you could check in a finished image.
* Imperfection is the realism: asymmetry, pores, uneven teeth, flyaways, creases, scuffed walls and clutter are what separate a real photo from an AI one. Write the flaws down as carefully as the features.
* Looseness: real bodies are relaxed unless they're doing something. Tension only where the action needs it.
* Locked, variable and drifting traits: mark each trait as **locked** (never changes: eye colour, moles, teeth, scar), **variable** (changes per post: outfit, makeup, hair up or down, tidy or messy room) or **drifting** (changes slowly over weeks: hair length, roots, tan, a new plant).
* Sides are named from the subject: "mole on her left cheek (right side of the picture)". Always give both, because image models mix them up.
* Consistency over novelty: real people rewear clothes, use the same mug and film in the same corner of the same room. Repetition is what makes an account feel like a real life.
* Words have a ceiling: a description alone won't make a render move like a person. Real human input (motion transfer from real footage, expression references), several takes per frame, edit passes and a strict reject checklist (Part 10) close the rest of the gap.
* Fictional only: the persona never resembles a real person or celebrity, and the home is never a real, findable address.

### 1.2 Where the pillar ends and the persona begins

* The pillar (from the source post) decides the format, the scene type, the pose, the outfit type and the hook.
* The persona file decides what the person and their places look like, and how this person moves, reacts and expresses things.
* The moment spec (Part 7) is written per shot: what is happening right now, and how the body and the place respond to it.
* When the pillar says "her bedroom", use the persona's bedroom spec. When the pillar is set somewhere the persona has no spec for (a beach, a hotel), describe that place fresh for the post and don't add it to the persona unless it will recur.

## Part 2: Physical Appearance

### 2.1 Identity basics

* Apparent age: one number plus a range (e.g. 31, reads 28 to 34). Adults only, never under 21 in appearance.
* Gender presentation.
* Heritage: as visible traits (skin undertone, eye shape, hair texture), not just a label. The label alone gives the model a stereotype.
* Height: exact (e.g. 5'8", 173 cm), and how it shows (taller than the door handle, ceiling clearance in a small flat).
* Build: slim, athletic, average, soft, stocky, muscular; where weight sits.
* Where they're from and where they live: affects grooming, clothes, skin (sun exposure) and the whole environment (Part 6).

### 2.2 Face shape and structure

* Face shape: oval, round, square, heart, long, diamond.
* Forehead: high or low, wide or narrow, lines.
* Hairline: straight, rounded, widow's peak, receding temples, baby hairs.
* Cheekbones: high, flat, prominent, soft.
* Jaw and chin: soft, angular, slightly square, pointed, receding, cleft; double chin when looking down.
* Proportions: long or short midface, wide or narrow set features.
* Profile: flat, straight, convex; how the nose and chin line up.
* Asymmetry: which side differs (one eye slightly smaller, one brow higher, a crooked smile). Every real face has some.

### 2.3 Eyes

* Colour: base colour plus detail (darker ring around the iris, lighter flecks, two tones), and how it changes in sun (4.3). Never "piercing" or glowing.
* Shape: almond, round, hooded, monolid, downturned, upturned, deep-set.
* Size and spacing: close-set, wide-set, average.
* Lids: visible crease, hooded, heavy.
* Lashes: length, density, straight or curled, natural or with mascara, a few clumped.
* Under-eye: shadows, slight bags, fine lines, puffiness in the morning.
* Whites: slightly pink or veined, not pure white.
* Glasses (if any): frame shape, colour, thickness, lens glare, the lens shrinking or magnifying the eyes slightly, nose pad marks when off.

### 2.4 Eyebrows

* Shape: straight, arched, soft angled, flat.
* Thickness and density: full, thin, sparse at the tail.
* Colour: same as hair, darker, lighter.
* Grooming: natural with stray hairs, brushed up, plucked, laminated.
* Marks: a gap, a scar through the brow, a cowlick.

### 2.5 Nose

* Bridge: straight, small bump, scooped, wide, narrow.
* Tip: rounded, pointed, bulbous, upturned, downturned.
* Width and nostril shape: narrow, wide, flared, visible from the front or not.
* Size relative to the face.
* Skin: redness around the nostrils, visible pores on the nose.

### 2.6 Mouth and lips

* Fullness: upper vs. lower (e.g. fuller lower lip).
* Width: relative to the eyes.
* Cupid's bow: defined, soft, flat.
* Colour and texture: natural colour, slightly dry, fine vertical lines, chapped corners.
* Resting position: corners neutral, slightly up, slightly down; lips closed or slightly parted.

### 2.7 Teeth and smile

* Colour: natural off-white. Never bright white veneers.
* Alignment: crowding, a small gap, one tooth slightly forward or rotated, worn edges. Say exactly which teeth (e.g. lower front teeth a little crowded, one upper canine slightly forward).
* Size and shape: small, square, rounded, longer front teeth.
* Gum show: none, some, gummy smile.
* Smile shape: closed-lip, open, full laugh, one-sided smirk; how wide this person's smile goes and which side lifts more. How smiles behave is in 3.2.
* Dimples: none, one side, both.

### 2.8 Ears

* Size and shape, how far they stick out, attached or detached lobes.
* Piercings: which ear, how many holes, which holes are empty.
* Visible or covered by hair by default.

### 2.9 Skin (face)

* Tone: descriptive (fair, light-olive, medium-brown, deep) plus Fitzpatrick type I to VI.
* Undertone: warm, cool, neutral, olive.
* Texture: visible pores (where: nose, inner cheeks, forehead), fine facial hair that catches the light, slight unevenness.
* Freckles: where, how dense, how dark (e.g. clustered across the nose bridge and upper cheeks).
* Moles and beauty marks: exact position and size of each, with both sides named (e.g. two small flat moles on her left cheek, one near the jaw).
* Redness: around the nose, on the cheeks, chin, rosacea, flush when warm.
* Breakouts: none, the odd spot (where it tends to appear), acne scars, healed marks.
* Pigmentation: sunspots, darker patches, uneven tone around the mouth or eyes.
* Shine: where the skin gets shiny (T-zone, nose tip, forehead) and when (afternoon, after a workout).
* Age signs: smile lines, crow's feet, forehead lines, neck lines, matched to the age.
* Scars: location, size, colour (raised, flat, paler or darker than the skin).

### 2.10 Facial hair (if any)

* Type: stubble, short beard, full beard, moustache, none (and how often they shave).
* Density: full, patchy on the cheeks, thin on the upper lip.
* Colour: including greys or a different colour from the head hair.
* Grooming: sharp line-up or natural edge, ingrown hairs, razor bumps.

### 2.11 Hair

* Colour: base colour, highlights or dye, roots growing out (how many cm), greys, and the colour it shows in sun (4.4).
* Length: tied to a body landmark (chin, shoulder, collarbone, mid-back), not "medium".
* Texture: straight, wavy, curly, coily (1A to 4C), strand thickness.
* Density: thick, average, fine; how much scalp shows at the part.
* Part: centre, off-centre, side; which side.
* Cut: layers, blunt, curtain bangs, fringe, fade, undercut; uneven ends if it's been a while since a cut.
* Condition: frizz, flyaways at the crown, split or dry ends, baby hairs, greasy at the roots on day 3.
* Default styles: down, low bun, claw clip, ponytail, braid; how messy each one is, and how it comes loose during the day (strands falling out of the bun).
* How it behaves: sticks to the neck when sweaty, falls in the face, tucked behind one ear (which ear), how it moves when she turns her head.
* Wash cycle: freshly washed (fluffy) vs. day 3 (flatter, oilier roots).
* Accessories: scrunchie, claw clip, headband, colour and material.

### 2.12 Makeup and grooming defaults

* Default level: none, minimal, everyday, full glam; when each is used.
* Products that show: tinted moisturiser, concealer only under the eyes, mascara, lip balm, brow gel.
* Coverage: skin texture must still show through any makeup.
* Nails: length, shape, bare or polished, chipped polish, cuticles, a hangnail.
* Body grooming: what is and isn't shaved, regrowth.

### 2.13 Body

* Height and frame: bone structure, shoulder width vs. hips.
* Build and muscle tone: where it's defined, where it's soft.
* Proportions: torso vs. leg length, arm length.
* Default posture: upright, slight slouch, head forward from phone use, one shoulder higher. How posture changes with mood is in 3.3.
* Body skin: tan lines, stretch marks, cellulite, visible veins, darker knees and elbows, body hair, bruises.
* Keep body descriptions neutral and non-sexual. No before and after bodies.

### 2.14 Hands and feet

Hands are the most common AI giveaway; specify them.

* Size and shape: long fingers, short fingers, broad palms.
* Knuckles and veins: visible, slightly wrinkled knuckles.
* Nails: see 2.12.
* Marks: calluses (gym, guitar), small scars, dry skin, a ring tan line.
* Feet (if they show): shape, nails, polish, dry heels.

### 2.15 Distinguishing marks

* Scars: where, size, shape, colour.
* Tattoos: design (original, never a brand or copyrighted art), placement, size, how faded, linework quality.
* Piercings: all of them, with jewellery type and metal.
* Birthmarks.
* Braces, retainer, hearing aid, glasses, contact lens tint.

### 2.16 Signature items (identity anchors)

Items they own and use in many posts, so the account reads as one person.

* Everyday jewellery: e.g. small gold hoops, a thin chain, a ring they always wear (which hand, which finger).
* Watch: type and strap.
* Phone: model, colour and case (it shows in every mirror selfie and person taking a selfie shot, so it must never change without a reason).
* Glasses or sunglasses.
* Bag, water bottle, headphones, keys and keyring.

### 2.17 Wardrobe system

Not specific outfits (those come from the pillar), but the closet they come from.

* Style: e.g. clean and minimal, sporty, thrifted, preppy, streetwear.
* Palette: the colours they wear most, colours they never wear.
* Fit: oversized, tailored, relaxed.
* Fabrics: what most of their clothes are made of (heavy cotton, thin jersey, knit, denim, linen), since fabric decides how it creases and moves (4.10).
* Price level: matched to the persona's life (no designer pieces on a student budget).
* Core repeat items: a list of 8 to 15 pieces that come back often (a grey hoodie, a black puffer, one pair of white trainers).
* Condition: pilling, a stretched collar, faded black, worn soles, the hoodie cuffs that are a bit frayed.
* Default lived-in level: how worn their clothes usually look, on the 1 to 5 scale in 4.10.
* Tiers: home, errands, going out, gym, sleep. List the items in each tier separately; a scene at home uses the home tier unless the post says otherwise.
* Home clothes are the least presentable: old printed tees (freebies, souvenirs, old events), faded or cracked graphics, holes along the hem or collar seam, stretched necklines, bleach spots, frayed cuffs with thumb holes, mismatched pyjamas and socks. Worn out, but washed: not ragged, not dirty. The AI default puts people at home in clean, plain, new-looking basics, which reads as a catalogue.
* Prints: describe each one (colour, graphic, how cracked or faded). Use original graphics; image models garble lettering, so keep printed text out or worn to illegible.
* Logos: none, or blurred, unless a paying brand.

### 2.18 States (variable per post)

Written once, used when the post calls for it.

* Morning: puffy eyes, pillow creases, flat hair, no makeup, slower movements.
* Post-workout: flushed cheeks and chest, sweat at the hairline and upper lip, damp hair stuck to the neck, heavier breathing.
* Tired: darker under-eyes, paler lips, heavier lids, sinking posture.
* After crying: red rims, pink nose, blotchy skin.
* After a shower: see 4.5.
* Dressed up: makeup on, hair done (still with skin texture).
* Seasonal: winter pale and dry lips, summer tan and freckles darker.
* Ill: red nose, chapped skin.

### 2.19 Drift timeline

A short log of slow changes, so posts a few weeks apart stay believable.

* Last haircut date and how long it has grown since.
* Roots growth (about 1 cm a month).
* Tan fading or deepening.
* Nail polish chipping across the week.
* A new piercing, a new item they now always wear.

## Part 3: Expression, Body Language and Movement

Part 2 says who they are. This part says how they are alive. Write the persona-level defaults once (how this person laughs, stands, walks); each shot then picks from them in its moment spec (Part 7).

### 3.1 Gaze

* Camera-look ratio: how often this person looks into the lens. For candid content, no more than about 1 frame in 3, and never every slide of a carousel. Formats built on eye contact (talking head, still-stare) are the exception, and even there the eyes leave the lens between sentences.
* Every look has a target: the phone screen, the mug, the window, the floor while thinking, someone off-frame (say where: left of frame, at shoulder height). Unfocused "looking at nothing" is a target too (zoned out, thinking).
* Selfies: real people look at the screen, not the lens, so the eyes land slightly below the camera. A gaze locked dead into the lens reads as AI.
* Mirror selfie: eyes usually on the phone screen or on the reflection's body, not into the mirror lens.
* Eyes and head point different ways: head turned to the window, eyes back to the camera; chin down, eyes up; head forward, eyes sideways.
* In-between moments: mid-blink, eyes half closed while laughing, eyes squeezed shut, a sideways glance, looking down mid-sentence.
* Video: the gaze shifts every few seconds; looks away when thinking or remembering, comes back to make a point. Blinks 15 to 20 times a minute, unevenly, more when talking, often on a head turn.

### 3.2 Facial expression

* Every expression has a cause: "mid-laugh at something said off-frame", not "smiling". The cause goes in the moment spec; the prompt describes only the visible result (Part 9).
* Mixed and in transition: a laugh fading, a smile while chewing, a mouth mid-word, a frown starting, a smirk being held back, surprise turning into a laugh. Aim for the moment just before or just after the peak, not the peak.
* Asymmetry: one side of the mouth higher, one eye squinting more, one brow up.
* Real smile vs. photo smile: a real smile moves the eyes (cheeks lift, lower lids push up, crow's feet crinkle). A mouth-only smile is the AI default and reads as posed.
* The whole face moves: forehead creases when the brows lift, the nose wrinkles in a big laugh or at a smell, the chin puckers when pouting or holding back, smile lines deepen, neck tendons show when laughing hard or straining.
* Mouth details: lips parted mid-breath, a lip bite, tongue against the teeth, mouth slightly open when concentrating, lips pressed when unsure.
* Resting face: what the face does when nothing is happening. Usually neutral or slightly serious, not a smile.
* Expression repertoire (persona-level): 10 to 15 expressions written for this person specifically, e.g. how she laughs, her deadpan, her disbelief, her thinking face, her annoyed face, her caught-off-guard face, her tired face, her "ugh" face. Note which pillar types each one fits.
* Video: expressions build and relax rather than switching on and off; small flickers come and go in under a second; when talking, the brows, cheeks and chin move too, not only the lips.

### 3.3 Body language and posture

* Weight: on one leg with the hip shifted, leaning on a counter, door frame or wall, slumped into the sofa, perched on the edge of the bed, one knee up. Not evenly on both feet, square to the camera, unless the format needs it.
* Asymmetry: shoulders at different heights, one arm busy and the other resting, head tilted, feet pointing different ways.
* Hands always have a job: holding the phone, a mug, tucking hair, a sleeve pulled over the hand, a hand in a pocket, fiddling with a ring, resting on the neck, scratching an eyebrow. Never both arms hanging the same way.
* Self-touch: people constantly touch their face, hair, neck and arms, especially when thinking, awkward, cold or tired.
* Looseness: relaxed joints, soft knees, a slight slouch, neck forward over a phone, shoulders dropping when relaxed. Tension only where the action needs it (gripping, lifting, reaching, bracing).
* Mood drives posture: tired sinks and rounds the shoulders; excited sits up, leans in, hands up; annoyed crosses the arms and leans back; awkward folds in and touches the face.
* Turned toward something: a body angled partly toward someone off-frame or toward a sound, not always square to the camera.
* Persona signatures: how this person stands, sits, leans, and what they do with their hands. Written once, reused.

### 3.4 Holding and using objects

* Weight changes the whole body: a heavy tote pulls one shoulder down and the body leans the other way; a laundry basket on the hip pushes the hip out; a full mug is held level and close; a phone held up for a selfie raises that shoulder and tilts the head.
* Grip: fingers wrap the way the object needs (two hands round a hot mug, a pinky under the phone, a loose grip on something light, white knuckles on something heavy); the thumb is placed somewhere real.
* Contact: hands press into soft things, a palm pushes the cheek up when leaning on it, sitting compresses cushions and wrinkles the duvet, objects cast small shadows where they touch.
* Use, not display: the object is being used (drinking, typing, opening, reading the label), not held up to the lens like an ad. For a paying brand's product the grip and angle stay casual (opened, half used, label partly turned).

### 3.5 Movement (video)

* Walk: speed, stride length, arm swing (how much, which arm swings more), head bob, bounce, heel strike, how they turn (head first or whole body) and how they stop.
* Personal tempo: fast and jittery, slow and loose, matched to the persona.
* Never fully still: breathing moves the chest and shoulders, small weight shifts, sway, fidgeting, adjusting clothes, touching hair, blinking.
* Secondary motion: hair, earrings, loose clothes and bag straps follow the body a moment late and settle after the body stops.
* Uneven timing: real movement speeds up and slows down, pauses and hesitates, with a small wind-up before a big move (a dip before a jump, a breath before speaking) and a follow-through after.
* Speech: the head nods and tilts on stressed words, brows move with emphasis, hands gesture slightly ahead of the words, a breath before sentences, the odd swallow.
* Where the motion comes from: a text prompt can't produce a person's own cadence. The reliable route is copying real human motion with motion transfer (`tools/motion.py` + `kie.py motion`, Kling 3.0 Motion Control) from the source clip or from a clip we film ourselves: real human timing in, the persona's face and body out.
* Persona motion clips (optional): one or two short clips, filmed by the team with the filmed person's consent, that define how this persona walks, sits down and talks, kept as motion drivers.

### 3.6 Voice (video only)

* Accent and region.
* Pitch, pace and volume.
* Tone: dry, warm, fast and excited, flat.
* Verbal habits: fillers, catchphrases, laugh sound, breaths and pauses.

## Part 4: How the Body Reacts to Its Environment

A real body is always being acted on: light lands on it, wind moves it, cold reddens it, a noise turns its head. A render without these looks like a person pasted onto a background.

### 4.1 One world state per shot

* Before describing the person, set the world: light sources and their direction, time of day, weather, wind (direction and strength), temperature, and what is going on around them (noise, people, smells).
* Everything agrees: the shadows on the face, the rim light on the hair, the shadows on the floor and the glare on the window all come from the same sun; hair, clothes, trees and steam all move the same way in the same wind.

### 4.2 Light on skin

* Direction makes shape: shadow under the brow, beside the nose, under the cheekbones, under the jaw onto the neck, in the collarbone hollows. Flat, even, frontal light is the AI default and flattens the face.
* Hardness: direct sun gives hard-edged shadows and bright highlights; overcast or indirect window light gives soft gradients; a bare overhead bulb darkens the eye sockets.
* Highlights: a sheen on the forehead, nose bridge and tip, cheekbones, upper lip and chin; on the shoulders and collarbones in sun. Real skin shines in particular places, never as an even matte or an even glow.
* Translucency: with light from behind, ears, nostril edges and fingers glow red-orange.
* Sun on skin: a warmer tone, more visible texture and fine hair, freckles stand out, squinting.
* Colour casts and bounce: warm bulbs turn skin orange, a screen puts blue on the face at night, grass and trees add green, a wooden floor adds warmth, a coloured top tints the chin and neck, mixed light gives each side of the face a different colour.
* Darker skin: highlights are sharper and more reflective and pick out the bone structure; never lightened or washed out in bright light.

### 4.3 Light in the eyes

* Catchlights: their shape and position match the light source (a window is a rectangle, a lamp a small point) and sit in the same spot in both eyes.
* In sun the pupils shrink and the iris looks lighter and more textured (brown goes amber or honey, hazel goes greener, blue goes paler); in dim light the pupils are big and the eyes look darker.
* Bright light makes people squint: lower lids push up, crow's feet show, a hand may shade the eyes, the head turns away.
* Wind and cold make the eyes water slightly; tiredness reddens the whites.

### 4.4 Light on hair

* Backlight: the outline of the hair glows and flyaways light up as single strands.
* Colour shifts: dark brown shows red or auburn in sun, black shows a blue or brown sheen, blonde breaks into several tones; the roots look darker than the sunlit lengths.
* Shine: smooth straight hair has one highlight band following the head's curve; wavy and curly hair has many small highlights.
* Shadow: hair casts shadows on the forehead, cheek and neck.

### 4.5 Wet and sweaty

* Wet skin: darker, shinier, with small sharp highlights, beads of water on the shoulders and running drops, goosebumps if cold.
* Wet hair: one or two shades darker, heavier, clumped into strands, stuck to the forehead, cheeks and neck, dripping at the ends; curls tighten; frizz comes back as it dries.
* Wet clothes: darker where wet, clinging and creased, heavier drape. Kept non-sexual.
* Sweat: sheen on the upper lip, temples, hairline, chest and back; darker patches on clothes (underarms, chest, lower back); damp baby hairs.
* Rain: drops on skin and hair, wet streaks on jacket shoulders, droplets on the lens.

### 4.6 Wind and air

* Hair blows the wind's way, strands cross the face and catch in the mouth, a hand pulls hair back or holds it down.
* Clothes press against the body on the side the wind hits and flap or billow on the other; open jacket edges lift.
* The eyes narrow, the face turns away from a strong gust, the shoulders hunch.
* Video: gusts come and go, so hair and clothes don't move at a constant rate.

### 4.7 Temperature

* Cold: red nose tip, cheeks, ears and knuckles, chapped lips, hunched shoulders, crossed arms, hands in sleeves or pockets, visible breath outdoors, layers.
* Heat: flushed face, sheen, damp hairline, fanning with a hand, hair tied up, sleeves pushed up, clothes sticking.
* Cosy indoors: blanket over the shoulders, socks, a mug held for warmth.

### 4.8 Sound, smell and other people

* Sound: the head turns toward a noise, a small startle, a laugh at something off-frame, eyes going up at a neighbour's noise.
* Smell and taste: a nose scrunch, leaning in to smell coffee, a grimace, eyes closing in enjoyment.
* The person holding the camera: a friend taking the photo gets a different face (relaxed, laughing, "stop it") than a phone on a timer or propped on a shelf. Decide who took the shot.

### 4.9 Contact with the environment

* Sitting and leaning deform things: cushions sink, the duvet wrinkles around the body, a wall takes the shoulder's weight.
* Contact shadows: under the feet, where a hand meets the table, where the back meets the sofa. Missing contact shadows make a person look pasted in.
* The environment marks the body: pillow creases on the cheek, sock marks on the ankles, a watch-strap mark, a dent from a hair tie on the wrist.

### 4.10 Clothes on a body

* Wrinkles follow the body: bunching inside the elbow, behind the knees, at the waist and across the lap when sitting; pull lines toward the shoulder when an arm reaches up; stretch across the back when hugging the knees.
* Fabric decides the drape: heavy denim holds its shape, thin cotton clings and creases fast, knit stretches and bags at the elbows, linen creases everywhere.
* Lived-in, not dirty: soft creases from wear, drawer fold lines on a fresh T-shirt, sleeves pushed up unevenly, a collar sitting a bit off-centre, a half-tucked hem, a stretched neckline, a little pilling or lint, a stray hair on a dark top. Clean, but worn.
* Lived-in level (per outfit, per shot): 1 = straight from the drawer (fold lines), 2 = just put on, 3 = worn half a day, 4 = worn all day, 5 = slept in. Never 0: ironed and unworn reads as a catalogue.
* Clothes move: they follow the body with a slight delay in video, shift when sitting (hoodie rides up, waistband folds over), and react to wind and water.
* Accessories move too: a necklace slides to one side, earrings swing, a bag strap twists.

## Part 5: How They Are Photographed

The same face looks AI-made or real depending on how it's captured. Define the persona's default camera.

* Device: their phone (e.g. a recent iPhone). Front camera: wider, slight face distortion when close, a bit softer. Rear camera: sharper.
* Who takes it: use the exact terms in Extraction Guidelines 2.17 (selfie, person taking a selfie, photo of a selfie, mirror selfie), and decide who pressed the shutter (4.8).
* Why now: the shutter was pressed because something happened (a laugh, a turn, a moment in the light), so the frame catches the person mid-action, not settled into a pose.
* Default distance and angle: arm's length, slightly above eye level, slightly tilted.
* Lighting: real household or outdoor light with a direction (Part 4), never studio softboxes or beauty rim light.
* Depth of field: phone photos are sharp most of the way back. Strong background blur reads as a pro camera or AI, unless it's a portrait-mode shot.
* Flaws to keep: slight sensor noise, a small focus miss, slight motion blur on a moving hand or hair in low light, a blown-out window, uneven white balance, mirror smudges and dust.
* Framing: imperfect crops (top of the head cut off, a door frame in the edge), slightly crooked horizon.
* Phone processing look: the slight sharpening and HDR of phone photos; no beauty filter, no skin smoothing. Phone-camera artefacts can be added in code after generation (P004 learnings).
* Check with numbers: compare sharpness, saturation and noise against the source with `tools/measure.py`. A soft, pale, clean render reads as AI.

## Part 6: The Environment They Live In

### 6.1 Location and context

* Country, city and neighbourhood type: inner city, suburb, small town, rural.
* Climate and season: affects the light through the window, the clothes on the chair, condensation, heating, and how the body reacts (Part 4).
* Time zone and shooting times: decides the light in each room (e.g. UK winter is dark by 4pm).
* Home type: flat, terraced house, studio, shared house, student halls, family home.
* Building age and style: Victorian conversion, 1970s block, new build. Shows in windows, ceilings, skirting and floors.
* Floor: ground floor, top floor; what the view out of the window is.
* Rent or own, and budget: everything in the home should match what they could afford.

### 6.2 Household

* Who else lives there: flatmates, partner, family, kids (never shown), pets.
* Traces of them: a second toothbrush, someone else's shoes, a shared fridge with labels.
* Pets: specified as fully as the persona (breed, colour, markings, size, collar), since they recur.

### 6.3 Floor plan

* List of rooms and which ones appear on camera (bedroom, kitchen, bathroom, living room, desk corner, hallway mirror, balcony, car).
* A simple map: which wall the window is on, where the bed, desk, mirror and door are. This keeps light direction and backgrounds consistent between posts.
* Camera spots: the 2 or 3 places the persona usually films or takes photos in each room, and what is behind them from that spot.

### 6.4 Per-room spec (repeat for each room that appears)

* **Size and shape:** small, narrow, high ceiling, sloped attic ceiling.
* **Windows:** number, size, type (sash, casement, sliding), direction they face, curtains or blinds (colour, half-broken slat).
* **Walls:** paint colour and finish, scuffs, Blu Tack marks, nail holes, a patch of different white where something was painted over.
* **Floor:** carpet, laminate, floorboards, tiles; rug (pattern, worn patch, curled corner).
* **Fixed details:** radiator or AC unit, sockets and switches (country-specific), skirting boards, door handles, smoke alarm, ceiling rose.
* **Furniture:** every main piece with style, material, colour, age and condition (flat-pack, second-hand, hand-me-down; a wobbly leg, a chipped edge, a drawer that doesn't close).
* **Lighting:** ceiling fitting (bare bulb, paper shade, flush light), lamps, fairy lights, bulb colour (warm 2700K, cool 4000K), and how daylight moves through the room during the day.
* **Bedding and textiles:** bedding colour and pattern, how many pillows, throw blanket, towels, how wrinkled.
* **Decor:** prints and art (original designs only, no copyrighted posters), plants (species, size, health; one that is a bit dead), books, photo frames (no real people's photos), candles, mirrors.
* **Colour palette:** 3 to 5 main colours of the room.
* **Clutter list:** what is lying around, by default. Chargers and tangled cables, hair ties, a half-drunk glass of water, mugs, skincare bottles, receipts, post, clothes on the chair, shoes by the door, a laundry rack, tote bags on a hook, an open drawer, a tissue box, a hoodie on the bed.
* **Clutter level:** 1 (tidy) to 5 (chaos), and the default. Never 0: a perfectly clean, empty room reads as a render.
* **Wear and dirt:** limescale on the tap, grout stains, dust on the shelf, a mark on the mirror, fingerprints on the fridge, a sun-faded book spine, a cable held with tape.
* **States:** tidy day, messy day, laundry day, evening (lamps on, blinds down), morning (blinds half open).

### 6.5 Rooms checklist (common sets)

* Bedroom: bed, bedside table and what is on it, wardrobe or clothes rail, the mirror they use for mirror selfies (full-length, leaning against the wall, smudged).
* Bathroom: sink, mirror, lighting over the mirror, products on the counter (generic or unbranded), towel, shower or bath, tiles, extractor fan, condensation.
* Kitchen: worktop material, kettle, toaster, fridge (magnets, notes), sink with a few dishes, cupboards, the drying rack, the window over the sink.
* Living room: sofa (colour, material, sag), coffee table and what is on it, TV or not, rug, plants.
* Desk or work corner: desk, chair, laptop (no visible logo or a generic one), monitor, sticky notes, cables, mug, lamp.
* Hallway or entrance: shoes, coats on hooks, keys, mirror, post on the floor.
* Balcony or garden: furniture, plants, view, what the neighbours' side looks like.

### 6.6 Signature objects (continuity anchors)

5 to 10 objects that appear in many posts so viewers recognise the place: a favourite mug, a specific throw blanket, the leaning mirror, a large plant, the bedding pattern, a lamp, a fridge magnet, a tote bag. Each one fully described (colour, material, size, wear) and drawn on a reference sheet.

### 6.7 Places outside home

Specify only the ones that will recur.

* Street outside: pavement, parked cars (no readable plates), house fronts, bins, trees, weather.
* Car: make level (not a named brand badge), interior colour, what's on the seats and dashboard, air freshener, phone mount. Matters for "selfie in a car" shots.
* Gym: type (big chain style, local, home gym), equipment colours, mirrors, lighting, flooring.
* Workplace, campus or office.
* Local café, park, supermarket, commute (bus, train, walking route).

### 6.8 Light and time

* Which direction each window faces and when direct sun comes in.
* Light quality per room per time of day: soft grey morning, warm low sun, dark evening with lamps.
* Night: warm bulbs mixed with phone and laptop screen glow.
* Season: winter grey and dark early; summer bright, late sunsets, blinds half down.

### 6.9 Regional realism

Small details that make a room read as a real country.

* Plug sockets and switches, radiators vs. AC, window types, door handles.
* Kitchen appliances: kettle (UK), rice cooker, electric fan, water dispenser.
* Packaging and products: generic or local-looking, no readable real logos unless it's a paying brand.
* Outside: street signs, road markings, plate style (unreadable), building materials, plants and trees that grow there.

### 6.10 Text and brand hygiene

* No readable real brand names or logos unless the brand is paying (then use their real assets, added in code where possible).
* Image models garble text. Keep books, packaging and screens with no readable text, and add any needed text in code afterwards.
* Screens (TV, laptop, phone) show something neutral or a generated app UI from the UI flow, never a real app with someone else's content.

### 6.11 Environment changes log

Dated record of what changed: a new plant, furniture moved, new bedding, the season's decorations, a move to a new flat. Posts must match the state of the home at their point in the timeline.

## Part 7: The Moment Spec (per shot)

Filled in for every frame or shot before writing its prompt. It turns "Willow in her kitchen" into a moment.

| Field | What to decide |
| :---- | :---- |
| Cause | What just happened or is happening (a joke, the kettle clicking, a draught, reading a text). |
| Action | What they are doing, as a verb, mid-way through (pouring, turning, pulling hair back, sitting down). |
| Gaze | Where the eyes are and why; camera or not (3.1). |
| Expression | Which repertoire expression, at which point (building, peak, fading), and its asymmetry (3.2). |
| Body | Weight, lean, shoulders, what each hand is doing (3.3). |
| Object | What they hold or touch, the grip, how its weight changes the body (3.4). |
| World | Light source, direction and hardness; weather, wind, temperature, noise (4.1). |
| Reaction | What the world does to the body: squint, rim light on the hair, flush, wind in the hair, wet strands (4.2 to 4.9). |
| Clothes | Outfit, lived-in level, where it creases given the pose (4.10). |
| Camera | Selfie term, who pressed the shutter and why now, distance, angle (Part 5). |
| Room | Room, camera spot, room state and clutter level (Part 6). |

Worked example (written for us, not the prompt):

* Cause: her flatmate, standing in the doorway, just said something funny; the flatmate is taking the photo.
* Action: leaning against the counter, waiting for the kettle, holding a hot mug.
* Gaze: toward the doorway, slightly left of the lens, eyes half closed.
* Expression: real laugh just past its peak, eyes crinkled, mouth open showing her slightly crowded lower teeth, left side of the mouth higher.
* Body: hip against the counter, weight on her right leg, head tipped back and to her left.
* Object: both hands round the mug at chest height, steam rising.
* World: low winter sun through the window behind her (her left, right of the picture), cold morning, kettle just boiled.
* Reaction: flyaways at the crown glowing copper, right side of her face (left of the picture) in soft shadow, slight redness on the nose.
* Clothes: grey oversized hoodie, lived-in level 3, sleeve pushed up unevenly on one arm, creased at the elbows.
* Camera: person taking the photo, rear camera, from the doorway, chest height, slightly tilted.
* Room: kitchen, doorway spot, morning state, clutter level 3 (crumbs, drying rack full, post on the counter).

## Part 8: Reference Set to Generate and Keep

Generated once with the best model (Nano Banana Pro, 4K; Seedream 5.0 Pro edits when skin and teeth come out too polished), stored in `personas/<id>/refs/`, attached to every image where they apply.

* Face: front, unretouched, neutral light.
* Character sheet: front, three-quarter, profile, laughing close-up showing teeth.
* Expression sheet: 6 to 8 of the repertoire expressions (3.2), including a real laugh, the deadpan, a sideways glance, a squint in sun and an eyes-closed moment, so the model sees this face moving.
* Light sheet: the same face in direct sun, soft window light, warm lamp at night and backlit, so skin, eye colour and hair colour under each light are fixed.
* Full body: front and side, in a plain outfit, to lock proportions.
* Hands close-up, including holding a mug and a phone.
* Hair: back and side view, one tied-up style, and wet.
* Each room: a wide shot from each camera spot, without the persona, at the default clutter level.
* Signature objects sheet.
* Floor plan sketch (can be a simple drawing, not an AI image).
* Motion clips (video): the persona motion clips from 3.5, if filmed.
* Costs and prompts in `personas/<id>/costs.json`.

## Part 9: Writing the Prompt and Generating

### 9.1 The prompt

* Prompt the moment, not the person: the reference images carry the face and body; the words go on the moment spec.
* Locked traits: one short line, the same every time. A long list of traits pushes the moment out of the model's attention.
* Describe only what is in the frame. Models draw what they're told about: "laughing at her flatmate off-frame" can put a flatmate in the picture. Write the result instead ("laughing, looking toward the doorway left of frame").
* Verbs over adjectives: mid-laugh, glancing, pushing her hair back, caught as she turns, reaching for.
* Say where she looks. Never leave the gaze to the model; its default is the lens.
* Order: camera, then the moment (action, gaze, expression, body), then the light and its effects, then the clothes, then the room and clutter.
* Words to avoid, because they pull toward the AI look: beautiful, perfect, flawless, stunning, glowing skin, posing, studio lighting, 8K, cinematic, model.
* Words that help: candid, unposed, caught mid-, unretouched phone photo, natural skin texture, visible pores, slight sensor noise, everyday clutter, lived-in, creased.
* Video (motion transfer): keep kie's minimal prompt; the moment comes from the starting still and the motion clip (P015 learnings).

### 9.2 Generating

* Several takes: at least 4 per frame; pick the most human one with the review in Part 10.
* Fix by editing: take the best take and edit it (move the gaze, add flyaways, crease the sleeve, add a squint, open the mouth to show the teeth) rather than re-rolling from scratch.
* Video starts from a still that already has the moment right (teeth showing if she'll talk or laugh, clothes creased, light directional).
* Real motion for bodies: use motion transfer from real footage for walking, dancing, sitting down and gestures (3.5).
* Phone artefacts and measuring: Part 5.

## Part 10: Realism Review (reject list)

Check every take before it's used. One hit is a re-roll or an edit.

### 10.1 Stills

* Looks straight into the lens when the format doesn't need it, or every slide of the carousel does.
* The smile doesn't reach the eyes; the face is perfectly symmetrical.
* Weight evenly on both feet, square to the camera; both arms hanging the same way; hands with nothing to do.
* Object held up like an ad; a grip that couldn't hold it; no weight showing in the body; no contact shadows.
* Light with no direction (no shadow side to the face); catchlights missing or not matching the window; shadows going different ways.
* Hair perfect: no flyaways, no strands out of place, no reaction to wind or light.
* Clothes uncreased, spotless, sitting perfectly; no wrinkles where the body bends.
* At home in clean, plain, new-looking clothes instead of the worn home tier (2.17).
* Skin the same texture and sheen everywhere; no colour from the light or the room.
* Background blur too strong for a phone.
* Teeth too white or too even; nails too perfect.

### 10.2 Video

* Moves at a constant speed: no pauses, wind-ups or settling.
* No blinks, or blinks at a perfectly regular rhythm.
* Hair and clothes don't move, or move without the body or wind causing it.
* The walk floats: no bounce, no weight transfer, feet sliding.
* Gaze locked on the lens the whole time.
* Only the mouth moves when talking.
* Face or teeth distort (`motion.py faces` flags them).

## Part 11: Guardrails

* Fictional only: no real person's face, no celebrity lookalike, no real influencer's room.
* Adults only, apparent age 21 or over.
* No real, findable address; street and window views stay generic.
* No body before and after content; style reveals are fine.
* No real people in photo frames, posters or screens in the background.
* Motion clips filmed by the team need the filmed person's consent; only their motion is used, never their face.
* TikTok's AI-generated content label always on when posted.
